Workload is extremely prefill-heavy (ISL:OSL ≈ 211:1) and near-totally prefix-reusable — a perfect prefix cache serves 98.4% of all prefill blocks, so the median turn re-prefills almost nothing. 1Workload shape

Real coding sessions: huge inputs, tiny outputs, very long conversations. Each turn resends the whole growing context, so input dwarfs output.

Finding 1 — prefill-bound. ISL mean 207,082 (p50 140,800, max 989,824) vs OSL mean 980 (p50 396). Ratio ≈ 211:1. Conversations run 165 turns on average (p50 61, max 3,225). The cost center is prefill, not decode.

2Cache reuse — near-total, and all prefix

Finding 2 — 98.4% of prefill is reusable prefix. Token-weighted reuse = 0.9836. The median turn's prefix-cache hit rate is 0.992 (mean 0.924). With a perfect cache the engine prefills only 1.6% of the input — mean 3,402 new tokens/turn (p50 1,344).
Finding 3 — it is all *prefix* reuse, and "of what" matters. Set-membership reuse equals contiguous-prefix reuse to 4 decimals (0.9836) — no out-of-order/middle reuse exists. Matching only the immediately previous turn captures 93.5%; the extra 4.9% needs a full per-conversation radix tree (earlier turns, after compaction/branch), not just "keep last turn's KV".
Finding 4 — block hashes are prefix-chained. Across 488k distinct (trace, block) entries, every block id has exactly one predecessor and one fixed position. So identical hash_id ⟹ identical entire prefix; identical content under a different prefix gets a new id. The corpus models true prefix-cache semantics — and cross-conversation reuse is 0 (scope=local), so concurrent lanes never share KV.

3KV footprint — sizes the offload pool

Finding 5 — per-conversation KV is large and long-tailed. Working set (distinct tokens over the whole session): mean 890,695, p50 331,392, p90 2,058,330, max 14,285,184. At ~34 KiB/token (est. DSv4 MLA @fp8): mean ~29 GB, p90 ~67 GB, max ~467 GB per conversation. With CONC concurrent lanes this overruns GPU HBM fast — the 2.5 TB CPU pool holds ~86 mean (or ~37 p90) conversations.

4Subagents — the KV stress case

Finding 6 — subagent fan-out is the worst case for the cache. 93/233 traces (40%) spawn subagents; mean 8.5 groups/trace (max 153), 29 inner requests/group. At a spawn point, groups fan out as concurrent child sessions (in-flight requests exceed CONC), and 13% of groups start COLD (fresh sub-task, zero prefix reuse) — a synchronized burst of fresh ~112k-token prefills. This is exactly when a finite GPU cache thrashes and offload earns the most.

5LMCache tool cache-simulator — realized hit rate vs capacity

The 98.4% reuse above is an infinite-cache ceiling. LMCache's own offline simulator (lmcache tool cache-simulator, lmcache-cli on CPU) replays the traces as lookup-hash logs through a finite LRU and reports realized token hit rate. Run on the full 233-conversation corpus, DSv4 MLA@fp8 (2.14 MiB/chunk).

Finding 7 — a finite cache must hold the working set, and concurrency multiplies it. Sequential (one conversation at a time) reaches the 98.4% ceiling only above ~40 GiB (≈ a conversation's working set); below that it collapses (19 GiB→75%, 9 GiB→40%, 1 GiB→0.3%). Interleaving CONC=32 concurrent conversations shifts the knee ~8× right (saturates ~340 GiB) — at a GPU-realistic 64 GiB the same workload drops from 98% to 17%. That gap is exactly the reuse a too-small GPU cache loses and CPU offload (LMCache/HiCache) recovers.

Finding 8 — at GPU-only capacity the cache thrashes. 64 GiB / CONC=32 over the full corpus produced 164M evictions; 81.9% of requests get 0% hit, the rolling hit rate (panel 4) sits at ~0.1–0.8% for most of the run before a tail recovery to 16.7%, and hits land deep in the LRU (panel 7: p90 position 25k of 30k chunks = nearly evicted). A prefix that survives sequentially is constantly evicted by the other 31 lanes — the mechanism offload fixes by spilling to DRAM instead of dropping.
